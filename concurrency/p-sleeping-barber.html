The sleeping barber

Ordering puzzlesmedium35 minDijkstra's sleeping barber, with several barbers and a closing timeRubrik

The sleeping barber problem is producer and consumer with a twist: when the waiting room is full, customers leave instead of waiting, and the barber must sleep without missing the next customer. Rubrik's debugging round has handed candidates a barber shop simulation to repair (a 2026 report), so know the clean version cold.

The task

A barber shop has a number of waiting chairs and one or more barbers. Customers and barbers are threads.

  • A customer calls getHaircut(id). If every waiting chair is taken (or the shop is closed), the customer leaves at once: the call returns false without blocking. Otherwise the customer sits down and waits; the call returns true only after a barber has finished that customer's haircut.
  • A barber loops: nextCustomer() sleeps while nobody is waiting (no busy waiting), then takes the customer who has waited longest out of the waiting room and returns their id. The barber cuts the hair (the test does this part) and then calls finishHaircut(id).
  • close() closes the shop: arriving customers are turned away, customers already waiting are still served, and once nobody is waiting, nextCustomer() returns -1 so every barber can go home.
  • The customer in a barber's chair no longer occupies a waiting chair. There may be several barbers; each customer is served by exactly one of them.

What the tests check

  • One customer, one barber: the customer gets a haircut and getHaircut returns true only after finishHaircut for that customer, not when the barber merely picks them.
  • An idle barber sleeps: after 300 ms with no customers the barber thread is blocked, not spinning (Java checks the thread's state, C++ the process's CPU time), and it wakes as soon as a customer arrives.
  • With 2 chairs and the barber busy, two customers sit down and the next ones leave at once with false. The seated ones are served in arrival order.
  • Burst: 10 customers arrive at the same instant at a shop with 3 free chairs and a busy barber, 15 times over (8 in C++). Exactly 3 sit down and exactly 7 are turned away, every time.
  • Two barbers cut two customers at the same time.
  • Closing: arrivals after close() are turned away, customers already seated are still served, and then every barber's nextCustomer() returns -1. No barber goes home while the shop is open.
  • A stress run with 2 barbers and 8 customer threads in random order: every customer who got true was cut exactly once and only after sitting down, nobody who got false was ever cut, nothing deadlocks. In C++, ThreadSanitizer must stay quiet.

Taught in: The sleeping barber and the unisex bathroom

Open them one at a time. Each one gives away a little more.

Write down the state: the queue of customers in the waiting chairs (at most waitingChairs long), which haircuts are finished, and whether the shop is closed. One lock guards all of it. Now list who waits for what: barbers wait for "someone is waiting, or the shop is closed"; a seated customer waits for "my haircut is finished".
getHaircut: under the lock, leave with false if closed or full; otherwise join the queue, wake one barber, and wait in a loop until your id is in the finished set. nextCustomer: wait in a loop while the queue is empty and the shop is open; return -1 if it is empty (so it must be closed), else pop the front.
Seated customers wait for different ids, so finishHaircut must wake all of them (or each customer gets its own condition). close must wake every sleeping barber, not one, so all of them can see the closed flag and go home.

What the interviewer asks after your first version works. Try to answer each one out loud before you open it.

Where is the lost wakeup in the classic sleeping barber, and why does your version not have it?
In a naive version the barber checks "nobody waiting", and before it actually goes to sleep a customer arrives, sees the barber "awake" (not sleeping), does not wake it, and sits down; then the barber sleeps forever next to a waiting customer. It happens when the check and the sleep are not one atomic step. With a monitor, the check and the wait happen under the same lock, and the wait releases the lock atomically, so the customer's signal cannot fall into the gap.
How would you build it with semaphores, as in the textbook?
A mutex protecting the waiting count, a customers semaphore the barber waits on (counting waiting customers), and a barber semaphore a customer waits on until the barber is ready. A customer takes the mutex, leaves if the count equals the chairs, otherwise increments it, signals customers, releases the mutex and waits on barber. With several barbers or a "done" handshake you need more semaphores, which is why the monitor version is easier to get right in an interview.
The customer must also know when the haircut is finished, not only when it starts. Why does that matter?
Because "the barber picked me" and "my haircut is done" are different events, and a customer that leaves at the first one walks out with half a haircut: in software terms, the caller resumes while the work it waited for is still running. Here the test cuts slowly on purpose, so a shop that wakes the customer in nextCustomer instead of finishHaircut is caught.
Should the shop be fair? Who gets the next free chair, and who gets the next barber?
The waiting room here is FIFO, so barbers serve in arrival order. Chairs are first come, first served by construction: whoever finds a free chair under the lock takes it. With several barbers, which barber wakes is arbitrary, which is fine because barbers are interchangeable. If customers had priorities, the queue would become a priority queue, and starvation of low priority customers would become the new follow up.
How is this related to a thread pool?
It is one: the waiting chairs are a bounded task queue, barbers are worker threads, a customer turned away is the pool's rejection policy, finishHaircut is completing the task's future, and close is a graceful shutdown that drains the queue. The same three bugs show up there: lost wakeups, signalling completion too early, and shutdown that strands sleeping workers.

Try for at least twenty minutes first. Reading a solution feels like learning; writing one is learning.

The idea

This is a monitor around a bounded FIFO queue with a twist on each side. Producers (customers) never block on a full queue; they are rejected. Consumers (barbers) block on an empty queue, and each item carries a completion that its producer waits for. The invariants: at most waitingChairs customers in the queue; every customer who got a chair is served by exactly one barber; a customer returns true only after its haircut is finished.

Why each line is there

  • Reject under the lock. "Is there a free chair" and "take it" must be one step; checking outside the lock lets two customers take the last chair.
  • Signal one barber on arrival. One customer can be served by one barber, and barbers are interchangeable. If no barber is asleep the signal is simply not needed: a busy barber re-checks the queue before it sleeps.
  • The barber waits in a loop on "queue empty and open". With several barbers, a woken barber may find that another took the customer first; the loop sends it back to sleep instead of returning -1 by mistake.
  • A finished set, and a broadcast in finishHaircut. Seated customers wait for different events. Waking all of them and letting each check its own id is correct; one condition per customer is the efficient version.
  • close broadcasts to barbers. Every sleeping barber must wake up to see the flag. Customers still in the queue are served first, because a barber returns -1 only when the queue is empty.

Cost and trade-offs

Every operation is O(1) under one lock, except the broadcast in finishHaircut, which wakes every seated customer (at most waitingChairs of them). With many chairs, give each customer its own condition, or a per-customer completion object (a future), and complete exactly that one.

How to say it in the interview

It is a bounded queue under one lock, where producers are rejected instead of blocking. A customer checks for a free chair and joins the queue in one step under the lock, signals a barber, and then waits in a loop until a barber marks its haircut finished. A barber waits in a loop while the queue is empty and the shop is open, then takes the oldest customer; after cutting, it marks that customer finished and wakes the seated customers. The lost wakeup from the textbook cannot happen because the barber's check and its sleep are one atomic step on the condition variable. Close sets a flag and wakes all barbers; they drain the queue and then get -1.
