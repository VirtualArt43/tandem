Company playbooks

Use reported interview formats to choose drills and follow ups, while keeping team differences and uncertainty in view.

Before this:How top companies test concurrencyHow to run a concurrency coding round

Concurrency appears in several places across the reports: a dedicated round, a follow up at the end of a coding round, a deep dive inside system design, or hardly at all. Knowing which one you face decides how you spend your last two weeks.

In plain words

Before a match, a cricket team reads scouting notes on the opposition: this bowler likes the short ball, this pitch turns after lunch. The notes are not promises; a bowler can surprise you. But a team that ignores them prepares for the wrong game.

These playbooks are scouting notes. Company attributions come from the research snapshot dated 15 October 2026, mostly anonymous candidate reports from 2022 to 2026; the preparation plans are our recommendations. Older examples are labelled. So read every "reported" as "this happened to someone", not "this will happen to you", and confirm the format with your recruiter.

How to read the evidence

  • Sources. The researchers read LeetCode Discuss and Blind posts and official prep pages. Additional evidence includes edited experience reports, commenter advice and community tags, labelled where used. Glassdoor, Reddit and most Medium pages were unavailable or not covered in some research passes.
  • Bias. LeetCode Discuss over-represents India loops and companies with machine coding rounds. Absence of reports is weak evidence.
  • Labels. "First-hand" means a candidate describing their own round. "Aggregated" or "self-reported database" means a list or community tag, a lead rather than proof. Linked drills practise the same skills; they are not claimed to reproduce the interview prompt exactly.
  • Team matters more than company at Google, Meta, Amazon, Apple and Microsoft. Ask which team and which rounds.

The source notes are docs/research/rubrik_databricks.md, google_uber_meta_others.md, rippling_and_design_rounds.md and problem_bank_and_languages.md. Their evidence tables retain source links, dates and confidence labels. The weights below are qualitative priorities inferred from that sample, not measured probabilities of receiving a question.

Rubrik

Format (reported). Dedicated "System Coding" rounds (also called Concurrency, Multithreading or Code Design), usually two per loop and often the first screen, 60 minutes in CoderPad or a similar tool that runs threads. Senior (G7, SSE) loops kept two concurrency-heavy code design rounds plus two system design rounds that also probed multithreading (chunked parallel file transfer, an scp-like copy). A separate 50 to 60 minute debugging round on someone else's multithreaded code appears in CPD, new grad and G5 loops, and once for an 8.5-year candidate. Online assessments: one coding problem plus about ten MCQs on multithreading and operating systems. Any language (Java, C++, Python and Go all appear). Redo rounds have been granted after a weak round.

What to expect. Runnable code, frequently with the high-level library banned (ExecutorService, BlockingQueue, concurrent queues; one 2026 report said "threading primitives only"). Interviewers then grade exactly what the library would have hidden: shutdown() versus shutdownNow(), interrupts, which exceptions you catch, per-task timeouts, idle thread reclaim. Fairness follow ups are explicit. One candidate saw the requirements change three times in one round.

Top problems, by number of reports. A dependency-aware parallel job runner that stops on the first failure (6 reports: dependency job runner); the debugging round (6: cinema, barber shop); the reader-writer family (4: read-write lock, searchers, inserters and deleters, an init, request and close wrapper); the multithreaded crawler (4: crawler); submit then wait for all (4: wait for all); file I/O with a concurrent follow up (pread and pwrite copy, cp over an offset API, byte I/O over blocks: parallel copy, block device I/O); queues inside a fixed buffer (3: ring buffer, two queues, one buffer); group admission with fairness (3: fair bathroom); newer in 2026, a bounded queue then a pool with shutdown (pool from scratch) and car and truck parking (parking lot). Other reported families: a thread-safe stack then CAS (lock-free stack), a buddy allocator (buddy allocator), seat booking, an LRU made thread-safe, rate limiters (none newer than 2022 in this research sample).

Emphasise. Hand-built primitives from locks and conditions, typed fast; lifecycle and exception semantics; fairness and starvation; code that compiles (one new grad fixed the deadlock but lost the round on a compile error); debugging speed. Prepare a deep systems project too: a senior loop was rejected by the hiring manager on project depth.

Uncertainty. One 2024 post was accused of being copied, though its bathroom problem was independently reported again in 2025. Some enginebogie records duplicate LeetCode posts with different level labels.

Databricks

Format (reported). The April 2025 official interview prep guide in the research snapshot lists a System Programming interview in the 4 to 6 interview panel: multithreading, synchronization, I/O, performance, files, buffering, caching and storage, single-threaded then multithreaded, graded on design quality, officially in pseudocode. Candidates call it the concurrency or multithreading round, at L4, L5, Staff and L7. Screens are almost always algorithms (an IP and CIDR firewall rule check is the most reported).

What to expect. Despite "pseudocode", one L4 had to write a full C++ implementation and another was told to get as close to working code as possible: plan for near-runnable code. Commenters on Blind said the concurrency round can stand in for system design at L4, with L5 adding system design (treat as unconfirmed commenter advice, not a level policy). One round was mostly theory: how ConcurrentHashMap locks and why HashMap is not thread-safe, with CAS questions said to be common.

Top problems. A concurrent, ordered, durable log or event writer: recurring requirements across reports include many producers, ordering, batching, fsync, completion after durability, flush, close and backpressure (3 first-hand in the notes, including an edited experience report: durable log writer); a web crawler, then "make it multithreaded", often with a depth limit (3: crawler); parallel chunk fetches behind a cache, each chunk fetched once (1: cached chunk reader); a lower-confidence PracHub report of a hit counter with a race follow up (hit counter).

Emphasise. I/O systems, not puzzles: group commit, ordering, durability before acknowledgement, shutdown without losing data. Say when threads help (I/O-bound waits) and how many. Correctness over cleverness: in one low-confidence report a lock-free attempt the candidate could not justify nearly sank the round. Java candidates: the memory model, happens-before and ConcurrentHashMap internals.

Uncertainty. Far fewer detailed public reports than Rubrik; several Blind questions about the round went unanswered.

LinkedIn

Format (reported). On the Systems and Infrastructure track (platform roles, Bangalore included), a one-hour round literally named "Concurrency", sometimes the first round, with working code. This is the most specific and best attested concurrency signal in the research, with many first-hand reports from 2022 to 2024. Phone screens add operating systems theory (process versus thread, mutex versus semaphore, paging) and have asked for the output of a multithreaded program; they work as elimination rounds. On the apps track and in the newer AI-enabled coding round, concurrency arrives as a follow up.

What to expect. A delayed scheduler in the shape of ScheduledExecutorService.schedule (one 2022 Bangalore candidate estimated about 6 in 10 rounds, not a measured company rate), a bounded blocking queue (the same candidate estimated about 3 in 10; one loop allowed only plain threads with wait and notify) with graceful shutdown, producer and consumer with shutdown, Building H2O, a ThreadPoolExecutor clone. Follow ups go into each small detail, including context switches. Staff loops still get thread-safety: a RetainBestCache with "an example of a race condition for each part you lock", and a sliding window rate limiter where you walk through two threads both seeing 99 of 100.

Top problems. Scheduled executor, bounded blocking queue, Building H2O, thread pool from scratch, sliding window rate limiter, delay queue.

Emphasise. Timed waits that wake early for an earlier task, shutdown, naming the race behind every lock. LinkedIn employees on Blind suggested Java; the C++ candidate should confirm the language early.

Uncertainty. Two 2025 infrastructure loops reported no concurrency round. Ask the recruiter for the exact round list.

Netflix

Format (reported). For platform, infrastructure, data platform, database and distributed systems teams, the recruiter announces a concurrency round, sometimes as the phone screen; one data platform onsite had concurrency coding, algorithms and system design. Product teams vary. No India Netflix loops were found in this research; that absence does not establish a hiring-location policy.

What to expect. Implement a scheduled thread pool executor; a concurrent hash map (one lock versus partitioned); a multithreaded client library. A reply offered preparation suggestions, without saying where each was asked: blocking and non-blocking task schedulers, a reader-writer lock, reentrant locks, an executor or connection pool, and "only three threads at once" with semaphores. Interviewers look for a producer and consumer queue, thread safety and proper signalling. Compile rules vary: one candidate was told no need to compile and to test verbally, another heard it should compile and run. A 2025 recruiter message recommended Java or C/C++, while allowing other languages.

Top problems. Scheduled executor, striped hash map, connection pool, read-write lock, reentrant lock.

Emphasise. Writing the executor yourself, cleanly, with a priority queue and timed waits, and explaining the signalling.

Confluent

Format (reported). From 2022 to 2024, many loops (India included) had a "Coding + Concurrency" round, nearly always a task or job scheduler that had to run. Since 2025, the US senior loops reported are two coding rounds, two system design rounds and values, with concurrency as a coding follow up. Code runs against tests on HackerRank. An insider's advice: know how the famous concurrency utilities work inside.

What to expect. A delayed task runner that wakes when a new task has an earlier deadline, scales from one consumer to many workers and shuts down cleanly (an aggregator page counts nine reports from 2020 to 2025). Follow ups: a random queue under many threads, a thread-safe LFU, locking in a qualifier round, a strongly versus eventually consistent expiring map; a movie ticketing design with concurrency issues. Several reports describe difficult system design rounds; the sample does not establish which round causes most rejections.

Top problems. Delay queue, scheduled executor, thread-safe LRU, thread pool.

Uber

Format (reported). The official Uber guide captured in the research names "Depth in Specialization (Coding 2)" without describing it and never mentions concurrency or machine coding. In India that slot is usually machine coding or LLD: runnable, modular code, now sometimes in person in Bengaluru. In the US it can be a plain algorithms problem, with concurrency as a follow up in another coding round ("how would this change in a multithreaded environment?", "10,000 concurrent requests: what is the bottleneck?").

What to expect. Concurrency is judged even when the prompt does not mention threads. Senior India reports: an expiry counter judged on production behaviour (purging stale data), car rental reservation, multi-producer multi-consumer queues (rejected for over-complication and no running code), a folder path printer called concurrently, a scheduler. The larger SDE2 (L4) pool, mostly from India: a multithreaded job scheduler with prerequisites, multithreaded pub-sub, "Kafka with concurrency support", a thread-safe leaderboard (answered with a keyed executor), a circuit breaker, cab hailing, a cache with expiry; and a US L4 depth round asked for a ranged key-value store with fine-grained locking. Design: a single-node cache centred on locking strategies, Splitwise with concurrent expenses, stock alerts with an idempotency key.

Top problems. Pub-sub broker, dependency job runner, scheduled executor, TTL cache, striped hash map.

Emphasise. A running demo with a driver that fires concurrent requests; separation of concerns; locking only what the invariant needs (an Uber interviewer in a 2021 Blind discussion complained of candidates who "put synchronization everywhere"); keyed executors and per-key locks.

Apple

Format (reported). High but team dependent, strongest for systems, cloud and frameworks teams. Some loops have dedicated concurrency rounds (a staff loop with two; a dining philosophers question; a thread-safe concurrent hash map; a scheduler; a thread-safe library). Others ask follow ups on ordinary problems (LRU, hit counter, BST iterator, elevator), fix concurrency bugs in existing code, or write concurrency inside system design as real Java code. A London candidate said concurrency came up in almost every round.

What to expect. Java teams ask internals: CompletableFuture, ExecutorService, ReentrantReadWriteLock, virtual threads; a 2026 hiring manager screen announced memory model, threading model and GC topics. coreOS teams pair concurrency with C, debugging and low level design. A 2026 AI-assisted round asked to make the code thread-safe.

Top problems. Thread-safe LRU, concurrent hash map, dining philosophers, hit counter, future from scratch.

Microsoft

Format (reported). Moderate overall, high in specific orgs (Azure Storage, Hyderabad teams, C++ systems teams such as the SQL engine). A Microsoft employee on Blind said it depends on the team and is rare for new graduates.

What to expect. An LRU or key-value store, then "make it thread-safe", sometimes "then distributed"; LLD judged on multithreading (a message queue, a cron or Airflow style scheduler); classic puzzles (print 1 to 100 with three threads, odd and even); build a primitive (a semaphore, a lock library, a read-write lock without library classes); read or debug concurrent C++ (predict the output, find the bug in a circular buffer's locking); theory down to CPU caches (volatile on one core versus many, synchronized internals); a parking lot answered with pessimistic versus optimistic locking.

Top problems. Thread-safe LRU, round robin printer, semaphore, read-write lock, scheduler, parking lot.

Nvidia

Format (reported). Moderate to high for system software roles, especially in India; US generalist senior loops leaned on project deep dives, C++ trivia and performance instead. Some India system software rounds were reported as C only.

What to expect. Theory: volatile, spinlocks, interrupt handlers, mutex versus semaphore versus spinlock, deadlock and lock ordering, process synchronization. Small C thread programs: even and odd printing with a mutex and condition variables, 1 to N with N threads (a candidate used N semaphores and reported a follow up asking for one counting semaphore; that report is not a proof that a semaphore alone enforces turns). A design round on Kubernetes operators asked how the race between threads reconciling the same object is handled. A thread-safe reference-counted shared pointer appears only in prep banks: plausible, not proven.

Top problems. Round robin printer, zero, even, odd, semaphore from scratch. Practise the pthreads section of the C++ cheat sheet, including what macOS lacks.

Rippling

Format (reported). A technical phone screen (HackerRank CodePair, one multi-part question, elimination), a hiring manager or department screen, then an onsite (often in person in Bangalore) with two or three coding rounds and one system design. Seniors may get a 90-minute machine coding or "web API" round; 2025 and 2026 loops added an AI-assisted coding round. Staff loops: system design, 90-minute coding, a project deep dive. Rippling reuses a small pool of practical, multi-part object-oriented problems: an in-memory key-value store with transactions, delivery cost tracking for drivers, music analytics, an expense rules engine, a spreadsheet, article voting.

What to expect. Concurrency is interviewer dependent. It was asked as a follow up on the key-value store (two threads updating one key: reproduce the race, then fix it), as "how to handle race conditions" on currency conversion, and as a discussion of options and trade-offs in the 90-minute API round. Some prompts exclude it explicitly ("does not need to be thread-safe"). In system design, hotel reservation is a standard question where interviewers pressed on handling multiple bookings for a set of nights, and idempotency recurs: event rollups judged on idempotency key design, a pay_up_to that must not pay twice, a hiring manager asking how you implemented idempotency. No Rippling "bug squash" round was found; that name belongs to Stripe.

Top problems. KV store with transactions; the hotel case in design cases; idempotency.

Emphasise. Running code with tests (one screen said non-running code is a reject), finishing all parts quickly, extensible object design, exact money handling (integer minor units or Java BigDecimal, with explicit scale and rounding), and saying if you have seen the question before (an offer was withdrawn over that). Mention thread safety, offer it, do it when asked, but do not slow the base solution.

Google

Format (reported). No dedicated concurrency round was found as a standard requirement for generalist L5 loops in this sample. In the research's scan, 8 of 128 senior-titled Google reports mentioned a concurrency term at all. That measures mentions in selected posts, not the probability that a loop tests concurrency. It shows up as the last 10 to 15 minutes of a coding round, after the main problem.

What to expect. "Several threads update this per-user counter map"; "one thread changes flight prices while many read them"; "many threads read and write this circular buffer"; "a multithreaded version of the time-based key-value store"; video frames arriving out of order (solved with mutexes); an async API that calls back on another thread. Dedicated concurrency questions are rare but real: a custom CompletableFuture (2026), a C++ multithreaded pub-sub queue (2020), a timer multiplexed over one hardware timer. Embedded, Android and infrastructure teams lean on it more.

Top problems. Concurrent time map, ring buffer queue, striped hash map, future from scratch.

Emphasise. Precise reasoning. The 2025 L5 candidate who named ConcurrentHashMap, AtomicInteger and synchronized still got No Hire and concluded that reasoning about races and guarantees was expected. Finish the main problem fast enough to reach the follow up.

Meta

Format (reported). No unambiguous first-hand concurrency coding report was found for generalist software engineering loops in this sample: coding rounds are two problems in about 40 minutes, judged on speed and clean code (one ambiguous "can you implement thread safe" follow up in 2025). Concurrency lives in system design and product architecture, in the specialist single-node design round (a 2025 recruiter note said it could cover concurrency and multithreading), and in embedded and iOS loops.

What to expect. Contention: ticket booking and Ticketmaster with "really deep dives into concurrency control and race condition resolutions" (an infra E5 round), seat locking, popular events; a high-contention question where the interviewer wanted one specific mitigation; auctions in aggregated question lists; concurrent contests; a single-machine memcache where the praised answer was sharding the table about one part per core to lock more efficiently.

Top problems. Seat booking, striped hash map; read design cases (seats, flash sales, the single-machine cache, auctions) and optimistic versus pessimistic.

Emphasise. One concrete mitigation per contention pattern: holds with expiry, conditional writes, optimistic versus pessimistic and when, queues and waiting rooms for hot events, lock granularity on one box.

Amazon

Format (reported). No dedicated concurrency round was identified as standard in this sample. At L6, system design and the Leadership Principles dominate, and the "Logical and Maintainable" coding round is graded on clean, readable code. In the scan, 1 of 35 senior-titled reports mentioned concurrency; this is a mention count, not an interview probability.

What to expect. LLD and design prompts with contended resources, mostly in India SDE2 loops: a concurrent parking garage, BookMyShow style seats (how to update the seat map when someone else books the seat you are choosing), the last unit of inventory ordered by two users at once, a locker system with distributed locking, a rate limiter with Redis Lua scripts for atomicity. One online assessment had a multiple-choice code review of a mutex-guarded C-style container.

Top problems. Parking lot, seat booking, TTL cache; read design cases and distributed locks.

Atlassian

Format (reported). Concurrency is a follow up in Code Design and coding rounds, often discussed rather than coded. The official guides captured in the October 2026 research describe Code Design as an AI-enabled round on an existing codebase, with no mention of concurrency.

What to expect. A rate limiter (fixed versus sliding window, then per user, then credits) with "why ConcurrentHashMap instead of HashMap" and synchronized; rating systems, a router and a Snake game ("concurrent button presses") with "how would you handle this in a multithreaded environment". One principal candidate's offer to add thread safety was declined by the interviewer. Candidates report that reasoning was weighed over completeness.

Top problems. Sliding window rate limiter, token bucket, hit counter.

Emphasise. A two-minute answer ready: a concurrent map plus a per-key lock or atomic, and the race it prevents; tests and extensibility first.

Stripe

Format (reported). Multi-part coding (in one report, Java on HackerRank), Bug Squash (failing tests and a few injected bugs in a real library: Python requests, Mako), an Integration round, system design, a manager round, and in 2026 an AI programming round. Asked directly whether there were concurrency questions, one candidate said there were none.

What to expect. The research found no verified concurrency coding, thread-safety follow up or race-condition bug in Bug Squash. Prep-site promises of those formats are unsupported by this sample; absence of a report does not rule them out. Idempotency is the realistic exposure: one research pass found no first-hand evidence, while a 2026 onsite report describes the Integration round as replaying requests, detecting duplicates and processing each once.

Emphasise. Idempotency keys and retries (idempotency), and methodical, narrated debugging of real code.

Turn the format into a correctness argument

Whichever company asks "make it thread-safe", start with the invariant, not the library name. For a fixed budget of 100 admissions with 99 already accepted, two callers must not both take the last place. This is the check-then-act pattern behind the reported LinkedIn 99-of-100 follow up; the following is a teaching example, not a reproduced interview solution.

Specify the operation. tryAdmit() consumes one admission and returns true, or returns false without changing state when the budget is exhausted. It never waits for a future admission; it can still block acquiring the mutex. There is no release or window reset here, so this small example is not a complete rate limiter.

The linearization point of success is the increment under the lock; failure takes effect at the locked full-budget check. Separate locks around a read and an increment would reopen the gap. An atomic load followed by an atomic increment also leaves that logical race; a compare-and-swap loop must combine the decision with a conditional update.

Prepare the next question. If the interviewer adds independent users, use one protected state per key while making creation and eviction of that state safe. If the limit is global across processes, a process-local mutex is insufficient: use an authoritative atomic operation in shared storage or explicitly partition the budget. A new window adds time and expiry semantics; a rollback adds idempotency. Explain the new invariant before changing the lock.

Say it out loud

Interviews are spoken. Answer each question out loud as if the interviewer just asked it. Tandem transcribes you on this Mac, word for word, measures your pace, fillers and pauses, and Bar Raiser scores what you said and how you said it.

Out loudabout 90 s
"You cannot use library executors or blocking queues here. How would you structure a worker pool, and what would you clarify before coding?"
What a strong answer covers
  • Start with a bounded queue, workers, one state lock and condition variables
  • Clarify submission, overload, completion and allowed primitives before coding
  • State admission and shutdown invariants and their shared synchronization
  • Explain predicate waits, task execution outside the lock and exception delivery
  • Distinguish draining shutdown from cooperative cancellation and termination
  • Give targeted tests for blocked callers, failure and shutdown races
How a senior would say it
I would build a bounded task queue and a fixed set of workers, with one lock protecting the queue and lifecycle state. Before coding, I would confirm which primitives are allowed, whether submit blocks or rejects when full, and whether callers need a future for results. I would also separate draining shutdown from immediate cancellation. My admission rule is that a submission either becomes accepted under the state lock or fails without entering the queue. Shutdown changes that same state under the same lock, so no task slips through an unprotected gap. Workers wait in a predicate loop for work or shutdown. They remove work under the lock and execute it outside the lock, so slow tasks do not block queue operations. Task failures reach the future or an explicit error handler, and ordinary task exceptions do not silently kill a worker. Draining shutdown rejects new work but finishes accepted work. Immediate shutdown removes pending work and requests cooperative cancellation of running tasks; it cannot safely force arbitrary code to stop. I would then wait for termination separately. My tests would cover capacity one, a throwing task, a submit racing shutdown, and shutdown with callers already blocked. Every test needs a time bound and checks for lost or duplicated work.

Likely follow up: How would you define wait-for-all if other threads keep submitting tasks while it waits?

Out loudabout 75 s
"Two requests both see 99 admissions out of 100. Walk me through the bug, your fix, and what changes for several servers."
What a strong answer covers
  • State the invariant and the check-then-act interleaving that admits two requests
  • Protect check and increment together, identifying the success and failure linearization points
  • Explain why separate atomic operations or a concurrent map alone do not fix it
  • Choose per-key synchronization only when the budget is per key
  • Use shared authoritative admission or partitioned budgets across servers and test the boundary
How a senior would say it
The bug is that the capacity check and admission are separate, so the system can accept two requests when only one place remains. Both callers read 99, both decide there is space, and both report success. Even if the stored count ends at 100, we have admitted 101 requests in total. I would protect the check and increment with one lock. Success takes effect at the increment, and failure takes effect at the protected full-budget check. The second caller then sees 100 and fails. An atomic load followed by an atomic increment still has the decision gap; a compare-and-swap loop can close it. A concurrent map alone also does not make a sequence on one entry atomic. For independent user budgets, I can move to per-key state, provided its creation and eviction are coordinated. Across servers, a local lock cannot enforce one shared limit. I need an authoritative atomic admission operation or an explicit partition of the budget. I would test the last-slot race and explain the behavior when that authority is unavailable.

Likely follow up: How would you add expiry without resetting the same user's budget twice?
