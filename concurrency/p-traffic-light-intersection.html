Traffic light controlled intersection

Ordering puzzleswarm-up20 minLeetCode 1279

A small state machine shared by many threads. The trap is a check then act split across two locks: protecting the light but not the crossing lets the light change under a car that is still in the middle of the road. The follow ups turn it into group mutual exclusion with fairness.

The task

Two roads cross. Road A (roadId 1) carries cars in directions 1 and 2; road B (roadId 2) carries cars in directions 3 and 4. Each road has a light, and exactly one road is green at a time. At the start, road A is green and road B is red.

Every car calls carArrived from its own thread. It receives two callbacks:

  • turnGreen switches the light to green for this car's road (and so to red for the other). Call it only when this car's road is red: turning a light green that is already green is a wrong answer.
  • crossCar makes the car cross. Call it exactly once, and only while the car's road is green.

Cars from different roads must never be in the intersection at the same time, and the light must not change while a car is crossing. Every car must get across: no deadlock.

Rules: any number of cars may arrive at once, from any number of threads; a thread may also send several cars one after another. In Java you may add throws InterruptedException if your version waits on a condition. In C++, ThreadSanitizer must stay quiet.

What the tests check

  • The light starts green on road A: a lone car on road A crosses without calling turnGreen, and a lone car on road B calls it exactly once, before crossing.
  • From one thread, cars on roads A, A, B, B, A, B, B, A call turnGreen exactly 4 times, once at each change of road.
  • Stress: 8 threads send 2,000 cars on random roads (seeded), and 100 cars arrive at the same instant, one thread each. The tests' crossCar takes a few microseconds, like a real crossing. Checked: nobody crosses on red, cars from both roads are never in the intersection together, the light never changes while a car is crossing, turnGreen is never called on a road that is already green, every car crosses exactly once, and nothing hangs.
  • No exceptions.

Taught in: Making threads take turns

Open them one at a time. Each one gives away a little more.

What state does the intersection have? One fact: which road is green. Every car reads it and some cars change it. What must be true about the read, the switch and the crossing?
"My road is red, so turn it green, then cross" is a check-then-act. If another car can act between the check and the crossing, the light can change under you. Hold one lock across all three steps.
A synchronized method (Java) or one std::lock_guard over the whole body (C++) is a complete answer: inside it, if the green road is not yours, call turnGreen and record the switch, then call crossCar. It is deadlock free because there is only one lock.

What the interviewer asks after your first version works. Try to answer each one out loud before you open it.

Your version lets one car cross at a time. Let cars on the same road cross together.
Count the cars crossing right now. A car on the green road joins at once (crossing += 1) and crosses outside the lock; a car on the red road waits until crossing == 0, then turns its road green and joins. The last car out signals the waiters. It is the readers and writers pattern with two groups of "readers" that exclude each other, sometimes called a light switch. The notes have the code.
In that version, can a road starve?
Yes, that is starvation. While cars keep arriving on road A, crossing never reaches zero and road B waits forever. Fixes: once a car is waiting on the red road, stop admitting new cars on the green road (like a writer-preferring lock), or let at most k cars through per green phase, then switch if the other road is waiting. Both trade a little throughput for bounded waiting.
Why is calling turnGreen for a road that is already green a real bug, not just wasted work?
A switch is an action, not a harmless write: a real controller runs an amber phase and a safety gap for it, so a duplicate command is not free. In code it also shows that two cars made the same decision from the same stale view, which is the check-then-act race itself. Making the transition idempotent (only switch when the state says red) under the lock fixes both.
What if the light must also change on a timer, say every 30 seconds, even with no cars?
Add a controller thread that sleeps until the next deadline and then switches the light under the same lock, but only when no car is crossing; it waits on the same condition otherwise. Cars on the red road then wait for the light instead of switching it themselves. The intersection becomes a small state machine with one writer for the light and many readers.

Try for at least twenty minutes first. Reading a solution feels like learning; writing one is learning.

The idea

The only shared state is which road is green. One lock is held across the whole decision: read the light, switch it if this car's road is red, and let the car cross. The invariant is while any car is crossing, the green road is that car's road, and it cannot change. Holding the lock through crossCar gives that for free, and with a single lock there is no lock order, so no deadlock.

Why each line is there

  • The check and the switch under one lock. Two cars on road B arriving together must not both see red and both call turnGreen.
  • The crossing under the same lock. Otherwise a road B car can switch the light while a road A car is still crossing. That is the most common wrong answer: it looks protected, because the light is.
  • Record the switch after calling turnGreen. The order does not matter inside the lock, but it reads naturally: the light changed, so the state says so.
  • carId and direction are unused. Both directions of one road are green together, so only the road matters. Say so; interviewers check that you read the statement.

Pitfalls

  • Locking the light but not the crossing. The light is consistent, yet a car can still be in the intersection when the other road turns green. The tests' slow crossCar exposes it.
  • No lock at all. Two cars both see red and both call turnGreen; in C++ it is also a data race.
  • Calling turnGreen unconditionally. Switching a green light to green is wrong by the rules.
  • Starting with road B green. The statement fixes road A as green at the start.

How to say it in the interview

The only state is which road is green, and "if my road is red, turn it green, then cross" is a check-then-act, so I hold one lock across all of it: read the light, switch it only if it is red for me, and cross before releasing. That keeps the light fixed while any car is in the intersection, and with one lock there is no deadlock. If we want same-road cars to cross together, I'd count the cars crossing and let the other road switch only when the count is zero, and then I'd add a rule against starving the waiting road.
