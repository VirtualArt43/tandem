Inventory reservations

Thread-safe designshard60 minAmazonFlipkartSwiggy

A stock check alone cannot protect the last unit, and a hold that never expires can hide available stock forever. Reserve stock and the user budget together.

The task

Implement expiring stock holds with a required user credit check. One unit costs one credit. User and SKU IDs index fixed initial arrays, copied into each instance. A successful reserve returns a unique positive ID, deducting quantity from both free stock and free credit. Insufficient stock or credit returns zero and applies no debit; the initial expiry sweep may still refund older holds.

Rules

  • Quantity and TTL are positive signed 64-bit values. Reject a negative initial balance, an invalid user or SKU in reserve, or a nonpositive quantity or TTL with Java IllegalArgumentException or C++ std::invalid_argument. Diagnostic reads receive valid indices.
  • The injected nonnegative monotonic clock is thread safe, nonthrowing and nonreentrant. Deadlines use checked signed 64-bit addition of the clock value and TTL. Overflow throws Java ArithmeticException or C++ std::overflow_error before creating or debiting a new hold. A deadline equal to the signed maximum is valid. At now >= deadline, a pending hold has expired.
  • Confirm a live pending hold once; later confirms of that confirmed ID return true, including after its original deadline. Confirmation makes its stock sale and credit spend permanent. Confirm after expiry returns false and refunds a still-pending hold. Unknown and already refunded IDs return false.
  • Release a live pending hold returns true and refunds both resources once. Expired holds are refunded but release returns false. Unknown, already released and confirmed IDs return false. There is no refund of a confirmed purchase.
  • expire() sweeps holds whose deadlines have passed at its clock snapshot. Reserve and diagnostic reads run that sweep first; if time advances during an operation, a later sweep may be needed. With a frozen clock, expired holds cannot keep stock unavailable. No background thread is needed.
  • Every operation touching both resources acquires the user lock, then the SKU lock. Registry lookup must release its lock before acquiring either resource. With no expired holds blocking the initial sweep, different user/SKU pairs can progress concurrently. Call the nonthrowing, nonreentrant entered(user, sku) hook exactly once per valid reserve attempt after taking both resource locks and before checking balances. The hook may pause the caller; retain both locks while it runs.
  • Initial quantities and credits may be zero. Allocation succeeds and the number of reservations cannot exhaust positive IDs. Never reuse a successful ID within an instance. Terminal hold records remain for idempotency during the exercise; separate instances own separate state.

Worked example

A user with three credits reserves two units from a stock of four. Free credit is one and free stock is two. If the hold expires before confirmation, both balances return to three and four.

What the tests check

  • Exact fits, quantities larger than one, zero balances, independent stock and credit rejection paths, unique positive IDs and independent instances.
  • Unknown IDs, repeated release, confirmation after release, and confirmation that stays idempotent after the original deadline. Terminal records never refund a committed purchase.
  • Release just before, at and after expiry; lazy expiry through each of reserve, stock and credit as the first operation at the deadline; repeated explicit sweeps.
  • Invalid arguments, checked deadline overflow, a deadline equal to the signed 64-bit maximum, and debit and refund of a full-width quantity. Failed validation leaves the instance usable.
  • A release paused in the clock while eight other callers target its hold. Eight further rounds race confirm against release, then race confirm, release and sweep at expiry. Each hold has one terminal outcome and at most one refund.
  • Sixteen callers contend for four stock units; sixteen requests across two SKUs compete for one user's three credits. Independent pairs overlap under their two locks.
  • Eight seeded callers each perform forty mixed operations. Final stock and credit match the exact confirmed ledger. Bounded joins and watchdogs report stalled workers, and C++ also runs under ThreadSanitizer.

What the interviewer asks after your first version works. Try to answer each one out loud before you open it.

What do the interview reports emphasize?
Amazon asks about two buyers and one unit. Flipkart asks to avoid both overselling and underselling during flash sales. Swiggy reports emphasize multi-user stock depletion and inventory locking; the expiring-hold mechanics also appear in the Meesho report.
Why can expiry create underselling?
If abandoned holds never return stock, the system rejects purchases despite having no committed sale for those units. A lazy sweep, expiry queue or background worker must perform the refund exactly once.
How do you reserve several SKUs?
Lock the user first, then all distinct SKUs in increasing ID order, validate the complete basket and commit all debits together. Partial success needs an explicitly different contract.
How do you keep expiry scalable?
Use a deadline heap or time buckets and verify state under the same resource locks before refunding. Heap entries may be stale after confirm or release; they are hints, not permission to refund.
What changes with multiple application servers?
Move the stock and hold transaction into a shared consistency boundary, such as transactional rows or a suitable atomic storage operation. Retries need idempotency keys; local mutexes alone cannot enforce a fleet-wide invariant.

Try for at least twenty minutes first. Reading a solution feels like learning; writing one is learning.

Key idea and invariants

For each SKU, free units plus pending holds plus confirmed sales equals initial stock. The analogous user equation holds for credit. A hold's state transition and both resource changes occur while the same ordered pair of locks is held. Terminal records prevent double refund or double confirmation effects.

Why these primitives

Per-user and per-SKU mutexes protect the cross-resource decision without serializing unrelated pairs. The registry stores immutable coordinates and deadline; mutable state is inspected under both locks. C++ releases its registry mutex before taking resource locks, while publication may take the registry briefly inside the pair, so no reverse lock dependency is introduced. Clock injection gives deterministic expiry boundaries.

Pitfalls and broken schedule

The broken confirm checks only that a hold is pending, forgetting its deadline. A caller reaches checkout after expiry and permanently spends stock that should have become available again. Releasing only one resource or refunding after confirmation would also violate conservation.

Cost and interview explanation

For H retained records, the simple lazy sweep takes O(H) time and C++ uses O(H) temporary snapshot space in the worst case. Reserve and both diagnostics include this scan. C++ registry lookup and insertion take O(log H); Java hash lookup is expected O(1). Both versions retain O(H) hold state plus the user and SKU buckets. Lock waiting is additional. Production expiry indexes and deduplication retention reduce the scan and storage costs. Say: “I will make the hold transition, stock delta and credit delta one ordered critical section. Every path, including expiry, takes user before SKU.”
