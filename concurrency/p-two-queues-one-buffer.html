Two queues, one buffer

Thread-safe structureshard50 minRubrik systems coding follow upRubrik

The follow up to Rubrik's fixed-buffer queue: two FIFO queues share one buffer, each may grow into whatever space the other is not using, and nothing is wasted. It is a tiny memory allocator and a blocking queue at the same time, and it is where the earlier version's index arithmetic stops being enough.

The task

Build two independent FIFO queues of ints, queue 0 and queue 1, that store their items in one shared buffer of slots slots:

  • enqueue(queue, item) appends an item to that queue. It blocks only while every slot in the buffer is in use, whichever queue is using them.
  • dequeue(queue) removes and returns that queue's oldest item. It blocks while that queue is empty, even if the other queue has items.
  • size(queue) is the number of items in one queue; used() is the number of slots in use by both together.

Any free slot can go to either queue, so one queue may use the whole buffer while the other is empty. The usual design threads linked lists through the array: a next index per slot, a free list of unused slots, and for each queue a chain from its oldest slot (head) to its newest (tail).

Rules:

  • The buffer and any per-slot bookkeeping (such as next) are allocated once, in the constructor. No allocation after construction: no nodes, no boxing, no growing collections.
  • No fixed split: if queue 1 is empty, queue 0 may fill all the slots.
  • Every operation is O(1).
  • No library queues or deques. Use a lock with conditions.

What the tests check

  • One queue can take every slot. Then an enqueue to the other queue blocks, and wakes as soon as a dequeue frees a slot.
  • Consumers wake for their own queue: a consumer of queue 0 that is waiting gets a new queue 0 item even when a consumer of queue 1 has been waiting longer, and the queue 1 consumer keeps waiting until its own item arrives.
  • 20,000 random enqueues and dequeues on both queues from one thread, checked step by step against a model, so slots move from one queue to the other thousands of times. Each queue must stay FIFO, with the right size and used.
  • A stress run with 2 producers and 2 consumers per queue on a buffer of 4 slots: every item arrives exactly once, on its own queue, in order per producer; used() never goes above 4; both queues finish. A slot used by two items at once shows up here as a lost item plus a duplicated one.
  • No allocation after construction: 20,000 enqueue and dequeue pairs from one thread allocate almost nothing.
  • In C++, ThreadSanitizer must stay quiet.

Taught in: Queues inside a fixed buffer

Open them one at a time. Each one gives away a little more.

Treat the buffer as a pool of slots. Each slot is either free or holds one item of one queue. You need two O(1) operations: find any free slot, and find a queue's oldest item. What structure gives both, using only arrays?
Linked lists threaded through the array. next[i] is the slot after i. The free list is a stack of free slots; each queue is a chain from head (oldest) to tail (newest). enqueue pops a slot from the free list and appends it at the queue's tail; dequeue unlinks the queue's head and pushes that slot back onto the free list.
Two traps. In dequeue, read the item and move head to next[s] before you overwrite next[s] to link the slot into the free list. And for waiting, use one lock for everything, one condition for producers ("a slot is free") and one condition per queue for its consumers ("this queue is not empty"), so a signal never wakes the other queue's consumer.

What the interviewer asks after your first version works. Try to answer each one out loud before you open it.

Why not just give each queue half of the buffer?
A fixed split wastes space exactly when it matters: when one queue is busy and the other idle, half the buffer sits empty while producers block. The interview asks for space that shifts with usage and minimal waste, and the shared free list gives every slot to whichever queue needs it.
Can one queue starve the other?
Yes. A fast producer on queue 0 with a slow consumer can fill every slot, and then producers of queue 1 block even though queue 1 is empty. If that matters, reserve a few slots per queue and share the rest, or reject instead of blocking. It is the classic trade-off between utilization and isolation, the same idea as a bulkhead.
The real buffer is 1 GB and holds messages of different sizes. What changes?
Make the slots fixed-size chunks, say 4 KB, and chain a message across as many chunks as it needs; the free list hands out chunks. One Rubrik candidate answered with lists of fixed-size chunks. The waste is at most one partly filled chunk per message plus one next index per chunk, and every operation stays O(1) per chunk.
Two stacks can share an array by growing toward each other from the two ends. Why not two queues?
A stack's live region always touches its end of the array, so the free space stays in the middle. A queue consumes from its front, so its live region moves and wraps around, and two circular regions in one array would collide and need data to be moved. Linked slots avoid moving anything.
Could each queue have its own lock?
Yes, with care. The free list is shared, so it needs its own lock, and you must fix a lock order (queue lock, then free-list lock, never the reverse). Then an enqueue on queue 0 and a dequeue on queue 1 overlap except for the short free-list section. Producers still need a way to wait for a free slot, which now lives under the free-list lock. One lock is simpler and correct; split it only if measurements show contention.
How would you check that no slot is ever used twice?
Write an invariant checker that runs under the lock: walk both queues and the free list, mark every slot visited, and assert that each slot is seen exactly once and that the counts match size(0), size(1) and used(). Run it after every operation in a single-threaded fuzz test. Under concurrency, an overwritten slot shows up as one lost and one duplicated item.

Try for at least twenty minutes first. Reading a solution feels like learning; writing one is learning.

The idea

The buffer is a pool of slots, and three linked lists live inside it, threaded through a next array: the free list, queue 0 and queue 1. The invariant is every slot is on exactly one of the three lists, and size(0) + size(1) = used = slots minus the free count. Each operation moves one slot from one list to another in O(1): enqueue moves a slot from the free list to a queue's tail, dequeue moves a queue's head slot back to the free list.

  • Enqueue: wait while the free list is empty; pop slot s from the free list; store the item; set next[s] to none; link s after the queue's tail (or make it the head if the queue was empty); signal that queue's consumers.
  • Dequeue: wait while this queue is empty; take s = head; read the item; move head to next[s] (and clear tail if the queue became empty); only then push s onto the free list; signal the producers.

Why each line is there

  • One lock for all three lists. A slot moves between lists in every operation, so the lists form one invariant.
  • Producers share one condition. They all wait for the same thing, any free slot, so waking one with signal is enough and any of them can use the freed slot.
  • One condition per queue for consumers. A consumer of queue 0 cannot use an item of queue 1. With a single shared condition and signal, the wakeup can land on the wrong consumer, who goes back to sleep, and the right one never wakes:
  • Advance head before reusing next[s]. The same next field links a slot into whichever list it is on. Pushing s onto the free list first overwrites the link to the rest of the queue, and the queue jumps into the free list.
  • Allocate everything in the constructor. Two int arrays and a few ints: no per-item allocation, so the structure never produces garbage and never calls the allocator on the hot path.

Cost

Every operation is O(1) under one lock. Memory is the buffer plus one next int per slot. Producers of both queues and consumers of both queues all serialize on the one lock, which is the price of sharing the free list simply.

How to say it in the interview

I treat the buffer as a pool of slots and thread three linked lists through it with a next array: a free list and one chain per queue with a head and a tail. Enqueue pops a free slot and appends it to that queue; dequeue unlinks the queue's head and pushes the slot back on the free list. Every slot is on exactly one list, so space moves to whichever queue needs it and nothing is wasted. For threads: one lock, a not full condition shared by producers, and a not empty condition per queue so a signal never wakes the other queue's consumer. Everything is O(1) and nothing allocates after construction.
