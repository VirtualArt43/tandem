Producer consumer with shutdown

Thread-safe structuresmedium40 minLinkedInGoogleMicrosoftUber

Every candidate can make a queue block. This problem tests what happens at the end: how a consumer knows that no more items will ever come, why it must not stop one item too early, and how to stop everything at once without losing a single item. LinkedIn's dedicated concurrency round asks exactly this, with the library taken away.

The task

M producer threads and N consumer threads share one bounded buffer. The items must be stored in an UnsafeQueue that the harness gives you: a plain FIFO queue that is not thread safe. Your class makes it safe, bounds it, and adds the two ways a run can end.

  • put(item) adds an item at the back. While the buffer holds capacity items, the caller blocks. It returns true when the item was added, and false when the buffer has been shut down (also when the shutdown happens while the put is waiting).
  • producerDone() is called once by each producer after its last put.
  • take() removes and returns the oldest item, blocking while the buffer is empty. Once every one of the M producers has called producerDone() and the buffer is empty, it returns the end marker (an empty optional) instead, to every consumer, every time. A consumer must never get the end marker while an item is still in the buffer or while any producer is still active.
  • shutdownNow() stops everything at once: it wakes every blocked put and take, and returns the items that were never delivered, oldest first. After it, every put returns false and every take returns the end marker.

The queue you must store the items in. Two threads inside it at the same moment corrupt it; the harness notices overlapping calls and the largest size it ever reached.

A consumer's loop shows why the end marker matters: it is the only way the thread learns it may exit.

Rules, as in the real interview: in Java use only synchronized, wait and notifyAll (or notify). The java.util.concurrent toolbox is banned: no library queues, no ReentrantLock or Condition, no Semaphore, no executors. A July 2024 LinkedIn infra loop allowed only plain threads with wait and notify plus a graceful shutdown, and a 2023 loop asked for M producers and N consumers on top of a queue that was not thread safe, ending when the producers stop and the consumers drain. In C++ use std::mutex and std::condition_variable. Items are never null.

Where else it comes up: a Google London onsite in 2022 started with one producer and one consumer, then one producer and many consumers, then many of each; Microsoft has asked for a multi producer, multi consumer queue in C++; an Uber round in 2022 was "similar to producer consumer".

What the tests check

  • One thread: items come out in order, then the end marker, and later takes keep returning it.
  • put blocks on a full buffer and wakes after a take; take blocks on an empty buffer and wakes after a put.
  • No early end: with one producer finished and one still active, three consumers drain the buffer and stay blocked; only the last producerDone() ends them, and it must wake all three, which the test makes sure are already asleep.
  • Drain first: when every producer finishes before any consumer starts, the consumers still receive all ten items, in order, before the end marker.
  • shutdownNow wakes consumers blocked on an empty buffer and producers blocked on a full one, returns exactly the undelivered items oldest first, and rejects later puts.
  • 1x1 with capacity 1 (every handoff blocks), and 4 producers by 4 consumers with capacity 4 started in random order: every item exactly once, each producer's items in order, every consumer ends, and the unsafe queue never sees two threads at once or more than capacity items.
  • shutdownNow in the middle of a busy run: every item whose put returned true is either delivered once or returned once, and nothing returned is older than an item already delivered.
  • In C++, ThreadSanitizer must stay quiet.

Taught in: Producer and consumer with a bounded buffer

Open them one at a time. Each one gives away a little more.

Before any code, list the state a consumer needs to choose between "wait", "take an item" and "stop". There are three pieces: the items, how many producers have finished, and whether the buffer was shut down. All three live under one lock, and the unsafe queue is touched only while you hold it.
Write the wait conditions as loops. A producer waits while the buffer is not shut down and is full. A consumer waits while the buffer is not shut down, is empty, and fewer than M producers are done. When the loop exits, decide in this order: shut down means reject or end; an item present means take it; otherwise every producer is done and the buffer is empty, so end.
Every change that can let someone else proceed must wake them: a put, a take, a producerDone and shutdownNow. In Java all threads sleep in the monitor's single wait set, so notify() may wake a producer when only a consumer can move, and at the end of the stream it wakes one consumer and strands the rest. Use notifyAll(). In C++ you may keep two condition variables, but the end and the shutdown need notify_all.
Lock on a private object (private final Object lock = new Object()), not on this: a caller who synchronizes on your buffer could otherwise steal your notifications or block you. shutdownNow drains the unsafe queue into a list while holding the lock, sets the flag, then notifies everyone.

What the interviewer asks after your first version works. Try to answer each one out loud before you open it.

Why notifyAll and not notify, when only one item was added?
Because with synchronized there is one wait set for everybody. A notify after a put may wake a producer that is waiting for room; it finds the buffer still full and goes back to sleep, and the consumer that could have used the item sleeps on: a lost wakeup. At the end of the stream it is worse: producerDone must tell every consumer, and notify tells only one. With a Lock and two Conditions, or two C++ condition variables, each waiter kind has its own queue, so signal or notify_one is safe for items; the end and the shutdown still need the "all" version.
Use poison pills instead of producerDone().
Once every producer has finished (a coordinator joins them), put one pill per consumer. Each consumer stops when it takes a pill, and FIFO order guarantees every real item comes out first. It needs no change to the buffer, but someone must know both counts, the pill must be a value no real item can equal, and a pill put into a full buffer blocks like any item. The counter in producerDone avoids the coordinator and works when producers finish at different times.
Add a graceful shutdown() next to shutdownNow().
shutdown() sets a "closed" flag and notifyAlls: later puts are rejected, puts already blocked on a full buffer are rejected too, but consumers keep draining what is already inside and get the end marker only when it is empty. So the consumer's end condition becomes "empty and (all producers done or closed)". shutdownNow() differs in that it also empties the queue and hands the items back. This is the same split as ExecutorService.shutdown and shutdownNow, and a 2024 LinkedIn loop asked for exactly this kind of graceful shutdown.
The interviewer points at each line and asks what happens if the thread is switched out right there.
Walk it calmly. Switched out while holding the monitor: other threads block on entry, nothing is half done because every state change happens under the lock. Switched out inside wait(): the monitor was released atomically with going to sleep, so another thread can change the state, and on waking this thread re-acquires the monitor and re-checks the loop condition. Switched out just after notifyAll() but before leaving the block: woken threads move to the entry set and cannot run until the monitor is released. Switched out between take() returning and processing the item: the item belongs to that consumer now; the buffer's invariant is already restored. A 2023 LinkedIn interviewer asked exactly these context switch questions.
Start with one producer and one consumer. What changes for one producer and many consumers, and for many of each?
1x1: one lock and the two waits; even here if instead of while is wrong because wakeups can be spurious. 1xN: consumers compete for the same item, so a woken consumer may find it already taken (the while loop handles it), and the end must reach all N consumers (notifyAll). MxN: "done" becomes a count of finished producers instead of a boolean, per producer order is still kept because the queue is FIFO and each producer puts in order, but there is no order across producers. Google London asked this exact progression in 2022.
notifyAll wakes every thread on every put. Can you do better with only wait and notify?
Yes: use two private lock objects as separate wait sets, one for "not full" and one for "not empty", which is what Condition gives you. It is easy to get wrong, because a thread must hold the right monitor to wait on it and the state must still be guarded by one lock, so mention it rather than lead with it. A simpler saving is to notify only on the transitions that can unblock someone: after a put that made the buffer non-empty, after a take that made it non-full. Under heavy load the extra wakeups of notifyAll cost context switches, not correctness.

Try for at least twenty minutes first. Reading a solution feels like learning; writing one is learning.

The idea

This is the monitor pattern from the bounded queue, plus an end of stream. One lock guards three things that form a single state: the items, the number of finished producers, and the shutdown flag. The invariants are: 0 <= size <= capacity; a consumer receives the end marker only when the buffer is shut down, or when it is empty and all M producers are done; nothing is added after shutdown. Every method takes the lock, waits in a loop until it can act without breaking those invariants, acts, and wakes whoever may now be able to act.

Why each line is there

  • A count, not a flag, for finished producers. With M producers, the first producerDone must not end anything. The end condition is finished == M && empty.
  • Drain before the end. After the wait loop, check for an item before deciding to end. Checking "all producers done" first stops consumers with items still in the buffer, which is the most common wrong answer.
  • notifyAll in Java. A producer-consumer monitor built on synchronized has one wait set holding both kinds of waiter, so a single notify can be wasted on a thread that cannot proceed (lost wakeup), and the end of the stream must reach every consumer.
  • Re-check the shutdown flag after waking. A producer blocked on a full buffer wakes because of shutdownNow; it must return false, not add an item that shutdownNow has already decided is undelivered.
  • shutdownNow drains under the lock. Setting the flag and emptying the queue in one critical section means every item is either already taken by a consumer or in the returned list, never both and never neither.
  • A private lock object. If you synchronize on this, any caller who does synchronized (buffer) joins your wait set and your notifications.

Pitfalls

  • if instead of while around wait: spurious and stolen wakeups make a consumer call remove() on an empty queue.
  • Calling wait() or notifyAll() on a different object than the one you synchronized on throws IllegalMonitorStateException.
  • Touching the unsafe queue in size() or a debug print without the lock: it is a data race even if it "only reads".
  • Swallowing InterruptedException inside put or take. Let it propagate so the caller can stop; the buffer's state is unchanged because wait re-acquires the monitor before throwing.

Cost and trade-offs

Every operation is O(1) under one lock. notifyAll wakes all waiters on every change, which costs context switches under heavy load but never correctness; the C++ version with two condition variables wakes only threads that can proceed for ordinary items. Compared with poison pill shutdown, the counter needs no coordinator and no special value. Compared with an executor's graceful shutdown, shutdownNow here is the abrupt form: it hands back queued work and leaves items already taken to their consumers.

How to say it in the interview

I will guard three things with one private lock: the unsafe queue, a count of finished producers, and a shutdown flag. Put waits while full and not shut down; take waits while empty, not shut down and fewer than M producers are done. After the wait, take checks for an item before it decides to end, so consumers always drain first. Because synchronized gives me one wait set for producers and consumers, I use notifyAll after every change; with notify, the last producerDone would wake one consumer and strand the others. shutdownNow sets the flag, drains the queue into a list under the same lock and wakes everyone, so every accepted item is either delivered once or returned once.
