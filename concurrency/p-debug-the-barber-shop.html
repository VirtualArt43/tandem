Debug the barber shop

Bug huntssenior45 minRubrik debugging round reportsRubrik

A 2026 report describes a Rubrik debugging round built on about 180 lines of a multithreaded barber shop, with shared chairs, brushes, scissors and combs; the task was to fix its deadlocks and its incorrect shutdown, and leave it compiling and running. This version is shorter and keeps both kinds of bug.

The incident

The simulation team models a barber shop. Several barbers share one waiting room with a fixed number of chairs, and one pair of scissors and one comb: every visit needs both tools. A customer who finds every chair taken leaves. A barber with nobody to serve naps until someone arrives. At closing time the shop stops letting people in, serves everyone already waiting, and sends the barbers home; close() returns once they have all left.

What the team reports:

  • Simulated Saturdays, with many customers and a mix of haircuts and beard trims, freeze part way through the day. CPU drops to zero, the served count stops moving and the waiting room fills up. Days with only haircuts never freeze.
  • Closing time never ends, even on a quiet Monday with nobody in the shop. The run has to be killed, so the end of day report is never written.
  • On this Mac the C++ job can stall without any ThreadSanitizer warning. A clean race report has not explained the freeze.

Your job: find both bugs and fix each with the smallest correct change. Keep the public API, keep the tools shared (a visit holds the scissors and the comb for the whole visit), and keep the shop's one lock for the waiting room and the counters.

Service is the work of one visit; the tests supply it, and it runs while the barber holds both tools. Each accepted arrival must invoke it exactly once with the original customer id and beard value. Rejected arrivals must never invoke it. An id is an opaque integer: negative values and repeated ids are valid, and repeated ids represent separate visits.

  • barberCount and chairs are positive. The chair limit counts customers still in the waiting room; a customer removed by a barber no longer occupies a waiting chair, even while that barber waits for tools.
  • served() counts callbacks that have returned, never arrivals or callbacks still running. turnedAway() counts every rejected arrival, whether the room is full or the shop is closing or closed. Counter reads and arrivals must remain responsive while a callback is paused.
  • Arrivals and counter reads may run concurrently. close() rejects new arrivals once it changes the open state, drains every accepted visit, wakes all idle barbers and joins them. It must wait for an active callback. Calling it again after it returns is safe; in C++ destruction also closes and drains the shop.
  • One external caller invokes close() at a time. Callbacks return normally and do not call close(); callback exceptions and concurrent closers are outside this task. Java callers can receive InterruptedException from close(). Tests do not interrupt the barbers or the closer.
  • Several barbers may take customers before obtaining the shared tools, so callback start order across barbers is unspecified. Keep the waiting room and counters protected by the shop lock, and release it before acquiring tools or invoking Service.

What the tests check

  • Sequential visits preserve arbitrary ids, repeated ids and both service types, with exactly one callback per accepted arrival.
  • A held callback does not count as served, does not block arrivals or counter reads, and does not consume a waiting chair. With one barber and two waiting chairs, the next excess arrival is rejected and counted.
  • Six idle barbers all wake and leave at closing time. A repeated close preserves the counters.
  • While a callback holds the tools, closing starts rejecting arrivals even though chairs remain available. Close waits for that callback and drains every already accepted visit. Every rejection during and after closing is counted.
  • Four rounds of twelve simultaneous arrivals compete for one or three waiting chairs while the only barber is held. Exactly the available number enter; rejected visits never run.
  • Four concurrent producers submit 2000 alternating haircuts and beard trims to three barbers. Every payload and multiplicity matches, counters are exact, and callbacks never overlap. In C++, destruction without an explicit close also drains accepted visits.
  • Helper completion has a 700 ms bound, and the busy batch has a 1 s progress bound. Global watchdogs report a failed test and terminate the process after 3.5 s in Java or 5 s in C++. C++ runs under ThreadSanitizer; its silence alone does not prove freedom from deadlock.

Taught in: Debugging rounds: the bug in someone else's threaded code

Open them one at a time. Each one gives away a little more.

There are two freezes at two different times of day. For each one, picture the thread dump: what is every stuck thread waiting for, and which thread holds that?
A barber holds tools while serving. Write down, for each kind of service, which tools it picks up and in what order. What happens when two barbers start different kinds of service at the same moment? Why do days with only haircuts never freeze?
close() waits for every barber to leave. What must a barber get back, after it wakes from its nap or finishes a customer, before it can leave? Who is holding that while close() waits?

What the interviewer asks after your first version works. Try to answer each one out loud before you open it.

If joining under the lock is the problem, why does close() take the lock at all?
To change open under the same lock that a barber holds while it decides to nap. If the flag changed outside it, a barber could check "open, nobody waiting", then the flag flips and the notify fires, then the barber sleeps: a lost wakeup, and closing hangs again. Change the state under the lock and join outside it. Java notifyAll must execute while owning the monitor. C++ notification may follow the unlock: the predicate change and the wait still use the same mutex, so the wakeup cannot be lost in that gap.
The reported larger version also shares brushes and chairs. How would you extend this exercise to three bugs including shutdown?
Add a brush required by both services and plant a second inconsistent acquisition order involving it, alongside the scissors and comb cycle and the join under the shop lock. Trace the full resource graph: fixing one pair can leave another cycle. Give all resources a single order, or reserve a visit's complete resource set atomically, then separately prove that closing drains accepted work and joins outside the room lock. This compact starter contains two bugs; the extra resource cycle is an extension.
The shop buys a second pair of scissors and two more combs. What changes?
Each tool type becomes a pool, best modelled as a counting semaphore (or a tool rack guarded by one lock and a condition). Taking one of each in a fixed global order, scissors then comb, still cannot deadlock; taking them in different orders deadlocks exactly like the locks did, only less often. Taking the whole set a visit needs at once, under one lock, also works and avoids holding one tool while waiting for another.
How does this relate to the classic sleeping barber problem?
The classic version (Dijkstra) uses semaphores: one counting waiting customers, one for a ready barber, and a mutex around the free chair count; a customer who finds no free chair leaves. This one is the monitor version: one lock and one condition, barbers wait while the room is empty and the shop is open. The invariants are the same: never more waiting than chairs, a barber naps only when nobody is waiting, and nobody seated is abandoned at closing.
arrive calls notifyAll for every customer. Is that wasteful?
It wakes every napping barber for one customer; one takes the customer and the others re-check and nap again. For arrivals a single notify would do, because every barber waits for the same thing and any one of them can serve. Closing must wake all of them, and because closing and arrivals share one condition, notifyAll everywhere is the simple safe choice. With many barbers, use notify in arrive and notifyAll in close.
How would you find each bug from a thread dump or from ThreadSanitizer?
The tools deadlock: jstack prints "Found one Java-level deadlock", with one barber holding the scissors and waiting for the comb and another holding the comb and waiting for the scissors. On Linux, ThreadSanitizer builds with deadlock detection can report the inconsistent lock order even without an actual freeze. Apple clang's ThreadSanitizer on this Mac does not report lock order inversions; use the watchdog and thread stacks to diagnose the C++ hang. Neither planted bug requires a data race. The closing hang is quieter: the closing thread sits in Thread.join while holding the shop's lock, and the barbers are blocked on that same lock. The JVM's deadlock detector says nothing, because join waits for a thread, not a lock, so there is no cycle of lock owners.

Try for at least twenty minutes first. Reading a solution feels like learning; writing one is learning.

Bug 1: the tools are picked up in two different orders (a deadlock)

haircut takes the scissors, then the comb. trimBeard takes the comb, then the scissors ("comb the beard out first"). Each barber can hold one tool while waiting for the other, so two barbers doing different services can wait for each other forever: a lock order inversion, and with mutual exclusion, hold and wait, no preemption and now a cycle, all four conditions for deadlock. Days with only haircuts use one order, which is why they never freeze.

With three barbers it happens even more easily than the timeline suggests. While one barber works, a beard trimmer can pick up the comb the moment it is put down and then wait for the scissors; if a third barber starting a haircut gets the scissors first, the cycle is closed.

Fix: one global order for the tools, scissors then comb, in both services. In C++, std::scoped_lock both(scissors_, comb_) in both services is an equally good fix.

Bug 2: close() waits for the barbers while holding their lock (a hang at shutdown)

close() is synchronized (in C++ it holds a unique_lock on m_) for its whole body, including the joins. A napping barber woken by notifyAll must take the shop's lock back before wait() can return; a busy barber needs it to count the customer and to take the next one. close() holds that lock while it waits for them to leave, and they cannot leave without it. Closing an empty shop hangs every time.

Fix: keep the lock for changing the state and notifying, then release it and join. The rule is general: never wait for a thread, or for anything slow, while holding a lock that thread needs. Calling out with no lock held is the open call idea.

Why the rest is right

Barbers wait in a loop on "nobody waiting and still open", so a spurious wakeup or a customer taken by another barber just sends them back to nap. A barber leaves only when the shop is closed and the room is empty, so everyone seated at closing time is served. arrive checks open and the free chairs under the same lock, so nobody can sit down after closing. The tools are taken after the shop's lock is released, so a long visit never blocks arrivals. Increment served only after the callback returns; increment turnedAway on both rejection paths. Clearing the queue to make shutdown quick violates the drain contract, even when the deadlocks are fixed.

How to say it in the interview

Two bugs. The barbers share the scissors and the comb, but a haircut takes scissors then comb and a beard trim takes comb then scissors, so two barbers can each hold one tool and wait for the other; I make both services take the tools in the same order. And close joins the barbers while holding the shop's lock, but a barber needs that lock to wake up, count its customer and leave, so close waits forever even in an empty shop; I set the flag and notify under the lock, then release it and join. The lock is still needed for the flag, otherwise a napping barber could miss the wakeup.
