Circuit breaker

Concurrent systemsmedium55 minUberDatabricks

A breaker must reject quickly when a dependency is failing, then admit a controlled recovery trial. A late callback must not rewrite the decision made by a newer generation.

The task

Implement a concurrent CircuitBreaker with CLOSED, OPEN and HALF_OPEN states. The breaker starts closed. Each admitted callback runs exactly once outside the state lock. Return its value unchanged, including a Java null, or propagate its original exception. Admission rejections throw Rejected without invoking the callback.

Rules

  • All four limits are positive. The injected clock returns nonnegative monotonic millisecond ticks; readings and their differences fit signed 64 bits. It is thread safe, nonthrowing and nonreentrant. Do not read wall time or sleep to drive the state machine.
  • In CLOSED, count failed callback completions in the sliding interval (now - window, now], using the clock reading when completion is recorded under the lock, not when the callback was admitted. A success does not erase failures. The exact thresholdth counted failure atomically opens the breaker. Already admitted callbacks may still finish, but cannot add failures to the old generation after the transition.
  • In OPEN, reject new calls until elapsed time reaches cooldown. The first admission at or after that deadline starts a new HALF_OPEN generation.
  • A half-open generation admits at most trialLimit calls in total, not that many new calls whenever a trial finishes. If enough callers arrive, exactly that many may enter. Reject excess callers while the batch is pending. Close and clear failures only after all trialLimit trials have been admitted and all have succeeded. Any trial failure immediately reopens and restarts the cooldown.
  • Every transition creates a new generation. A result from an earlier generation still returns to its caller, but cannot alter current breaker state or failure history. Admission rejections are not dependency failures. Every exception thrown by an admitted callback counts as a failure, including Java checked exceptions, InterruptedException and Error, C++ exceptions outside std::exception, and a callback that itself throws Rejected. Preserve the original Java throwable or C++ exception type and payload.
  • snapshot() is a coherent diagnostic. Its failure count is retained history, pruned on closed admissions and current-generation completions, including successful completions, and frozen while open or half open. Its trial count records total admissions in the latest half-open batch, resets at the start of each batch, and clears on successful recovery. It does not advance time or trigger a transition. Callbacks finish eventually; the breaker provides no timeout, cancellation or fairness guarantee.

Worked example

With threshold two, window 100 and cooldown 10, failures at ticks 5 and 8 open the breaker at tick 8. New calls are rejected through tick 17; a call at tick 18 can reserve a half-open trial slot.

What the tests check

  • Sixteen admitted calls are held before failing; the third completion opens the breaker and the remaining thirteen cannot inflate its failure count.
  • At one tick before cooldown, a rejected call never invokes the callback. At the deadline, sixteen shuffled callers compete for exactly three trial slots, with all three callbacks overlapping. Another 256 recovery batches reuse sixteen workers to stress atomic admission and completion.
  • Two successful trials finish while a third is held. No replacement trial may enter, and the breaker closes only after the held trial succeeds.
  • A failed trial restarts the full cooldown. The next batch starts with a fresh quota, needs all its successes, and uses a new generation for each transition.
  • Failure timestamps use completion time. All failures exactly one window old expire, including history that expires during a successful callback. A success preserves recent failures.
  • Snapshots leave expired history and overdue open states unchanged. History remains frozen during recovery and clears when recovery succeeds. Clock arithmetic also runs near the signed 64 bit limit.
  • Return values, invocation counts and exception propagation are checked. Java includes checked exceptions, interruption and errors; C++ includes nonstandard exceptions. A callback throwing Rejected counts as a failure, while an admission rejection changes no counters.
  • An old closed-generation failure cannot affect a recovered breaker. Old successful and failed trials cannot alter a later half-open batch, but still deliver their own results to their callers.
  • Callback gates force the important interleavings. Bounded joins and watchdogs report stalled workers or callbacks executed under the state lock. C++ runs under ThreadSanitizer.

What the interviewer asks after your first version works. Try to answer each one out loud before you open it.

How does this differ from the reported Uber exercise?
The 2026 report combines a request-count rate limiter with OPEN, CLOSED and HALF_OPEN states and concurrency questions. This bank exercise explicitly uses failures in a window. Keep those policies distinct rather than treating all requests as failures.
What was reported for Databricks?
The 2023 interview report describes compiled, running circuit-breaker code for a leader-follower database. It does not specify the exact threshold or recovery policy; the policy here is the exercise contract.
Should successes reset the failure counter?
Only if the chosen policy counts consecutive failures. This exercise counts failures within a sliding time window, so an intervening success does not remove them.
How do retries and timeouts interact with the breaker?
Choose whether each attempt or the outer operation counts, and define a timeout outcome once. A late response must not complete the same attempt twice. Retry budgets, backoff and a bulkhead limit prevent recovery traffic from overwhelming the dependency.
Should breaker state be shared across hosts?
Local state is fast but each instance admits its own probes. Shared state can coordinate a fleet quota but introduces availability and consistency costs. Specify whether the threshold is local or global before choosing a protocol.

Key idea and invariants

Admission checks state and reserves a trial slot in one critical section. Completion checks both the admission state and its generation before changing anything. There are never more than trialLimit tickets in one half-open generation, and only its own results can close it. The opening completion is the exact failure that reaches the threshold; subsequent completions from that closed generation are stale.

Why these primitives

One mutex or synchronized monitor protects the state, timestamp deque, generation and trial counters as one invariant. The clock is read inside those critical sections so failure timestamps append in order. The callback runs after unlocking, permitting independent calls to overlap. Record completion once on both the success and exception paths, then preserve the result for its caller. An exception from admission must never be routed through completion. The injected clock makes exact expiration and cooldown boundaries deterministic without waiting for real time.

Pitfalls and broken interleaving

The broken implementation compares only state names. Call A starts in closed generation zero and pauses. Calls B and C fail and open it; a successful trial then returns it to a new closed generation. A finally fails. The state is again CLOSED, so the broken code counts A against the fresh history and opens too early on the next failure. A generation is needed even though every counter update is locked. Returning trial slots early and executing a callback under the monitor are separate mistakes.

How to say it in the interview

“I will define exactly which events count before writing the state machine. Admission returns a ticket for one generation. I execute the dependency outside the lock, then apply its result only if that ticket is still current.” Space is O(threshold) for retained failures, with amortized constant-time pruning; callbacks dominate latency without serializing one another.
