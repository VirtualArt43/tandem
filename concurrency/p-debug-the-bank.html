Debug the bank

Bug huntssenior65 minRubrik

Incident report: a bank service passes its single-thread tests, but concurrent traffic produces reconciliation alerts and requests that stop completing.

The task

The starter is an existing service, not a stub. Operations staff observed an account below zero after a traffic burst, a total-balance alert that disappeared on retry, and requests that stopped completing while a scheduled interest run was active. Unrelated account transfers also show correlated latency. Reproduce the symptoms and repair the service without changing any public API or removing the supplied instrumentation.

Required behavior

  • There are 2 to 1000 fixed accounts, numbered 0 to N - 1. Copy the opening balances; later changes to the caller's array or vector must not affect the bank. Opening balances are nonnegative and at most 10^12; operation amounts are positive and at most 10^12. Balances after transfers and the total may exceed 10^12, so use 64 bit arithmetic. Account zero funds a fixed promotional interest credit to every other account. Interest is a conserved transfer, not newly created money.
  • A withdrawal succeeds only with enough funds, deducts once and adds that amount to withdrawnTotal. A transfer between distinct accounts succeeds only with enough source funds and moves the amount once. Self-transfers with valid arguments return false. Insufficient funds return false without changing any balance or counter. An amount equal to the available balance must succeed.
  • interestBatch credits every recipient or none, funded entirely from account zero. If that reserve cannot pay all N - 1 recipients, return false without changes, even if it could pay some of them. An exactly funded batch succeeds.
  • balance(account) returns a coherent balance, and each balance operation and audit must be linearizable with respect to withdrawals, transfers and batches. audit returns a single consistent total. With only transfers and funded interest, it remains equal to the opening total. After completed withdrawals, balances plus withdrawn total equal opening money. withdrawnTotal counts money removed by successful withdrawals; completedTransfers counts successful calls to transfer, excluding self-transfers, rejections and interest batches. Both start at zero. Diagnostic counters are individually coherent, not a combined atomic snapshot with each other or the account balances; check reconciliation after callers finish.
  • Disjoint account transfers must overlap. Preserve account-level concurrency while meeting the behavior above. Invalid account IDs, amounts, account counts or opening balances throw IllegalArgumentException in Java or std::invalid_argument in C++ before any mutation, including when the source and destination IDs are equal. Operation counters do not wrap during the exercise. Java callers supply a nonnull opening array and probe; the C++ probe outlives the service.
  • BankProbe callbacks are nonthrowing, nonreentrant instrumentation at the named decision and lock boundaries. afterCheck runs after a successful withdrawal decision and before its debit; firstLock runs after the first account lock in a transfer, interest batch or audit that locks all accounts; transferLocked runs after acquiring both account locks and before checking funds; auditRead runs after including each account in the total. Keep them at their corresponding boundaries in the fix; they may pause to make incident schedules reproducible. The service does no timed work.

Expected behavior

Two concurrent requests to withdraw 80 from a balance of 100 must yield one success and a final balance of 20. Transferring 20 between two accounts containing 100 each must leave every audit total at 200.

What the tests check

  • Exact source and destination postings in both directions, rejected self-transfers and insufficient funds, spending an exact balance, and counters that count only successful operations.
  • Invalid amounts, IDs and opening balances throw before mutation. A valid call on another thread still works afterward. The maximum 1000 accounts, copied inputs and totals up to 10^15 exercise 64 bit arithmetic.
  • Interest batches reject a partly affordable batch without changes and accept an exactly funded batch. A seeded sequential model checks all balances and counters after 600 mixed operations.
  • A controlled withdrawal burst checks that two requests cannot spend one balance twice. A paused audit overlaps a transfer, and repeated snapshots stay constant during eight seeded transfer callers.
  • Instrumented transfers on disjoint account pairs must overlap. A controlled transfer and interest schedule checks progress and exact postings.
  • Eight concurrent withdrawal callers must produce an exact withdrawn total. A separate mixed workload reconciles every successful withdrawal, transfer and batch against each final account balance.
  • Concurrent ordered balance reads check that a debit is never followed by an older destination balance while all transfers move in one direction. C++ also runs under ThreadSanitizer. These stress histories supplement the controlled schedules; they do not prove correctness for every possible interleaving.
  • Probe setup failures and unexpected worker exceptions are failures. Timed joins and process watchdogs bound deadlocks and stalled workers.

What the interviewer asks after your first version works. Try to answer each one out loud before you open it.

What is reported about Rubrik’s debugging round?
The 2024 CPD report describes multithreaded banking code to explain, repair and optimize; the candidate proposed a read-write lock and concurrent hash map. These three planted defects are the bank row’s practice specification, not claimed as a verbatim copy of the interview code.
Would a concurrent account map solve these incidents?
The account set is fixed, and map thread safety would not make a balance decision and update atomic, coordinate several account locks or give a multi-account snapshot.
Why not synchronize every public method?
One global lock can restore safety but makes unrelated transfers wait. The existing transfer metrics lock already has that performance smell. Protect only the affected accounts and keep counters atomic or narrowly synchronized.
How do you diagnose a production stall?
Capture thread stacks and identify held and requested locks, then draw the wait-for cycle. ThreadSanitizer on this Mac does not diagnose potential lock-order inversions; the watchdog demonstrates an actual stalled schedule.
How would an audit scale without locking all accounts?
Use immutable ledger snapshots, a versioned snapshot protocol or a transactionally consistent database read. Independently reading atomic balances still does not create one consistent total.
How do retries change the money API?
Add an idempotency key and commit the operation record with both postings. A caller retry after a lost response must not create a second transfer or withdrawal.

Try for at least twenty minutes first. Reading a solution feels like learning; writing one is learning.

The three planted correctness defects

The withdrawal checks funds under one lock acquisition and subtracts under a later one. Transfer locks accounts in increasing ID order, while interest locks them in decreasing order. Audit reads individually protected balances without a common snapshot. These are the only planted correctness defects; the transfer-wide metrics lock is a separate performance smell.

Key idea and invariants

Hold one account lock across the withdrawal decision and mutation. Every operation acquiring multiple account locks uses increasing IDs, including interest and audit. Audit retains all those locks while summing, and balance takes the corresponding account lock. Locking writers alone leaves an unprotected reader, which is a data race in C++ and can expose an incomplete transfer in either language. Transfer modifies both balances while holding its ordered pair, so money cannot be lost and an observer cannot see half a transfer.

Why these primitives

Per-account mutexes preserve concurrency for disjoint transfers. Atomic increments record successful transfers and withdrawn amounts without serializing the operations themselves. An atomic load followed by an atomic store is still a racy read-modify-write and can lose a counter update across disjoint accounts. Ordered all-account locking makes the batch and audit straightforward, at the cost of pausing transfers while those operations run. The fix retains the original API and instrumentation.

For the audit failure, read account zero as 100, move 20 from zero to one, then read account one as 120. Each read is synchronized but the combined 220 never existed. The global transfer metrics lock does not repair that snapshot.

Cost and interview explanation

Withdraw and transfer touch constant accounts; audit and interest acquire O(N) locks and use O(N) temporary lock storage. These operation counts exclude time waiting for locks. Balances and totals stay within the initial money supply, at most 10^15; a 32 bit accumulator is insufficient. Say: “I will first reproduce each incident, then identify the exact atomic region and a single lock order. I will test an overlapping audit and a transfer/interest schedule, and finally remove the global serialization while proving disjoint transfers still overlap.”
